中心极限定理：平均值的形状

看见个体分布与样本均值分布的差别。

从同一总体独立抽 n 个值，算一次均值；反复进行，就得到样本均值的抽样分布。中心极限定理讨论的是这个均值的标准化分布。

本课程入门条件是独立同分布、有限且正的方差。随着 n 足够大，均值分布趋近正态，中心为 μ，标准差为 σ/√n。原始总体不会因此变正态。

“n≥30”是经验提示，不是普遍保证。强偏态、重尾、异常值或依赖会影响近似。实验固定用指数总体：它始终右偏，均值直方图才随 n 改变。

E(X̄)=μ; SD(X̄)=σ/√n
(X̄−μ)/(σ/√n) ≈ N(0,1)
  • iid：独立且同分布
  • X̄：重复抽样中的随机样本均值

逐步推导：每一步怎么来

精确均值方差与极限定理推演

  1. 条件：X₁,…,Xₙ独立同分布，E(Xᵢ)=μ，0<Var(Xᵢ)=σ²<∞。X̄=ΣXᵢ/n，所以E(X̄)=Σμ/n=μ。

  2. 独立使交叉协方差为0，故Var(X̄)=Σσ²/n²=σ²/n，SD(X̄)=σ/√n。这两条对每个n都精确成立，不需要正态。

  3. 先看入门思路：平均仍围绕μ，但单个观测的正负偏差会部分抵消，波动按√n缩小。要证明形状趋于正态，需要下面的进阶工具；这不是“n≥30必然准确”的规则。

  4. 进阶工具定义：i²=−1，特征函数ψV(t)=E[e^(itV)]；它像分布的编码。取Y=(X−μ)/σ，则E(Y)=0、E(Y²)=1。

  5. 有限二阶矩给ψY(u)=1−u²/2+o(u²)。理由：e^(iv)=1+iv−v²/2+v²r(v)，r(v)→0且在实数轴有界；令v=uY，余项除以u²后为E[Y²r(uY)]，由支配收敛趋于0。

  6. 令Zn=ΣYᵢ/√n。独立给ψZn(t)=[ψY(t/√n)]ⁿ=[1−t²/(2n)+o(1/n)]ⁿ→exp(−t²/2)，使用(1+a/n+o(1/n))ⁿ→eᵃ。

  7. exp(−t²/2)是N(0,1)的特征函数，且在0连续。应用特征函数连续性定理，Zn依分布收敛到N(0,1)，所以对每个实z，P(Zn≤z)→Φ(z)。这里明确使用支配收敛与连续性定理作为分析学前提。

  8. 于是(X̄−μ)/(σ/√n)对足够合适的n可用标准正态近似；有限n误差仍取决于总体形状。若原总体正态，则线性组合本身正态，无需等极限。

计算题：从已知到答案

μ=10、σ=6，独立同分布抽取n=36。求均值标准误，并在正态近似适用时求P(X̄>12)。

  1. E(X̄)=10，Var(X̄)=6²/36=1。

  2. SE=√1=6/√36=1。

  3. 标准化阈值z=(12−10)/1=2。

  4. 近似右尾1−Φ(2)≈0.022750132；若总体强偏或重尾，不能仅凭n=36保证这个精度。

分步例题

总体均值 10、标准差 6，独立抽 n=36 个值。

  1. 样本均值的中心仍为 10。
  2. √36=6。
  3. 标准误为 6/6=1。
  4. 若正态近似合适，均值超过 12 的概率约为标准正态超过 2 的概率。
  • 样本均值的波动减小，不代表每个个体的波动减小。

CLT: the shape of sample means

See how the distribution of individuals differs from that of sample means.

Independently draw n observations from one population and compute a mean. Repeat to form its sampling distribution. The CLT concerns the standardized distribution of these means.

Introductory conditions are iid observations with finite positive variance. For sufficiently large n, sample means are approximately normal with mean μ and SD σ/√n. The original population does not become normal.

n≥30 is a rule of thumb, not a universal guarantee. Skewness, heavy tails, outliers and dependence affect approximation. The experiment uses an exponential population, which stays right-skewed while the means change shape.

E(X̄)=μ; SD(X̄)=σ/√n
(X̄−μ)/(σ/√n) ≈ N(0,1)
  • iid: independent and identically distributed
  • X̄: random sample mean across repeated samples

Step-by-step derivation

Exact moments and a limit-theorem argument

  1. Assume iid observations with mean μ and variance 0<σ²<∞. Then E(X̄)=Σμ/n=μ.

  2. Independence gives Var(X̄)=σ²/n and SD(X̄)=σ/√n exactly for every n, without normality.

  3. Intuitively averaging preserves the center while offsetting deviations reduces spread. Establishing a normal limiting shape needs the advanced argument below, not a universal n≥30 rule.

  4. Advanced notation: i²=−1 and ψV(t)=E[e^(itV)] is a characteristic function encoding a distribution. Set Y=(X−μ)/σ, so E(Y)=0 and E(Y²)=1.

  5. Finite second moment gives ψY(u)=1−u²/2+o(u²): write e^(iv)=1+iv−v²/2+v²r(v), with bounded r(v)→0. Dominated convergence applied to E[Y²r(uY)] makes the scaled remainder vanish.

  6. For Zn=ΣYᵢ/√n, independence yields ψZn(t)=[1−t²/(2n)+o(1/n)]ⁿ→exp(−t²/2).

  7. The limit is the standard-normal characteristic function and is continuous at zero. The characteristic-function continuity theorem yields Zn⇒N(0,1), hence P(Zn≤z)→Φ(z). Dominated convergence and the continuity theorem are stated analytical prerequisites.

  8. Thus the standardized sample mean admits a normal approximation when adequate. Finite-n accuracy depends on the population. For an originally normal population the result is exact at every n.

Calculation problem and worked solution

Take n=36 iid observations from a population with μ=10 and σ=6. Find the mean's SE and approximate P(X̄>12) when the normal approximation is adequate.

  1. E(X̄)=10 and Var(X̄)=6²/36=1.

  2. SE=6/√36=6/6=1.

  3. z=(12−10)/1=2.

  4. The approximate tail is 0.022750132. A strongly skewed or heavy-tailed population may make this inaccurate even at n=36.

Worked example

A population has mean 10 and SD 6; draw n=36 iid observations.

  1. The center of the sample means remains 10.
  2. √36=6.
  3. The standard error is 1.
  4. If the normal approximation is adequate, a sample mean above 12 corresponds to a standard normal above 2.
  • A less variable sample mean does not imply less variable individual observations.
